# Part 2 Introductions

# 2 sentences
- introduce the topic
- give a general answer

# Four types of question
- Discussion
- Opinion
- Problem and solution
- Two-part question


## Discussion
**Question**
> Some people think that it is **more effective for students to study in groups**, while others believe that it is **better for them to study alone**. Discuss both views and give your own opinion.
**Plan**
- Topic: study in groups or alone
- Answer: sometimes better alone, usually better in a group
**Introduction — paired against the question, line by line**
- [[\h:red:red|Some people think that ..., while others believe that ...]] → [[\h:red:red|People have different views about]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|more effective]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the effectiveness of]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|study in groups]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|group study]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|study alone]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|working alone]] / [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|studying independently]]
- [[\h:red:red|others believe that it is better for them to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|study alone]] → [[\h:red:red|While there are some benefits to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|studying independently]]
- [[\h:red:red|give your own opinion]] → [[\h:red:red|I believe that]]
- (nothing opposite) → [[\h:blue:blue|group work is usually more productive]]
**Whole introduction**
> [[\h:red:red|People have different views about]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the effectiveness of group study as opposed to working alone]]. [[\h:red:red|While there are some benefits to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|studying independently]], [[\h:red:red|I believe that]] [[\h:blue:blue|group work is usually more productive]].
**Reading the pairs**
- Row 1 folds the question's two clauses into one noun phrase — `as opposed to` is that `while` compressed into a preposition.
- Rows 2–4 turn adjectives and verb phrases into nouns (`more effective` → `the effectiveness of`, `study in groups` → `group study`), which is what lets a single `about` govern the whole comparison.
- Row 5 is the concession half of sentence 2: it takes the question's second view and hedges it (`some benefits to`), so row 6 still has somewhere to go.
- Row 6 has no topic words at all — the question's *instruction* `give your own opinion` becomes a frame.
- Row 7 is the only thing you add, and even that is hedged: `usually`, not `always`.

> ⚠ The answer stays **general**: `usually more productive`, not `always`. `sometimes better alone` is for the body, not the introduction.



## Opinion
**Question**
> Some people believe that **unpaid community service should be a compulsory part of high school programmes**. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
**Plan**
- Topic: community service for all teenagers
- Answer: three choices — agree, disagree, or balanced opinion (when possible)
**Introduction — paired against the question, line by line**
- [[\h:red:red|Some people believe that ...]] → [[\h:red:red|It is sometimes argued that]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|unpaid community service]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|some work in their local communities]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|compulsory part of high school programmes]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|high school students should be made to do]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|unpaid]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|unsalaried]] — held back for sentence 2, see the warning below
- [[\h:red:red|To what extent do you agree or disagree?]] → [[\h:red:red|I completely agree that]] / [[\h:red:red|In my opinion,]] / [[\h:red:red|While I ..., I do believe that ...]]
- (nothing opposite) → [[\h:blue:blue|the view itself]]
**Sentence 2 — three answers to the same question**
> [[\h:red:red|I completely agree that]] [[\h:blue:blue|this kind of scheme would be a good idea]].
> [[\h:red:red|In my opinion,]] [[\h:blue:blue|it would be wrong to force teenagers to do any kind of unsalaried work]].
> [[\h:red:red|While I disagree with the idea of]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|making such programmes compulsory]], [[\h:red:red|I do believe that]] [[\h:blue:blue|voluntary community service could benefit young people]].
**Whole introduction — sentence 1 fixed, sentence 2 one of the three**
> [[\h:red:red|It is sometimes argued that]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|high school students should be made to do some work in their local communities]].
**Reading the pairs**
- Rows 2–3: every noun the prompt supplies comes back reworded — `unpaid community service` loses its adjective and becomes the neutral `some work`.
- Row 4 has nothing to pair with in sentence 1: `unpaid` is the one word whose rewrite (`unsalaried`) is deliberately moved into sentence 2.
- Row 5 is where the colour flips — `To what extent do you agree or disagree?` is an *instruction*, not content, so it becomes a frame (`I do believe that`) rather than being paraphrased.
- Row 6 is the whole of sentence 2. The prompt states no view, so every blue block is yours, and the three versions differ only there.

> ⚠ The top sentence stays **neutral**: `some work`, not `voluntary`. The stance belongs to sentence 2 only — writing `voluntary` up front answers the question, and then the body has nothing to prove.


## Problem and solution
**Question**
> Many criminals **reoffend after they have been punished**. Why do some people continue to commit crimes after they have been punished, and what measures can be taken to tackle this problem?
**Plan**
- Topic: criminals reoffend
- Answer: several reasons, a variety of measures (governments, communities)
**Introduction — paired against the question, line by line**
- [[\h:red:red|It is true that]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|Many criminals reoffend after they have been punished]] → [[\h:red:red|It is true that]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|punishments do not always deter criminals from committing more crimes]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|reoffend]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|commit more crimes]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|criminals]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|offenders]]
- [[\h:red:red|Why do some people continue to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|commit crimes after they have been punished]] → [[\h:red:red|There are various reasons why]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|offenders repeatedly break the law]]
- [[\h:red:red|what measures can be taken to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|tackle this problem]] → [[\h:red:red|but governments could certainly take steps to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|address this issue]]
- (nothing opposite) → nothing added: this prompt asks for no opinion
**Whole introduction**
> [[\h:red:red|It is true that]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|punishments do not always deter criminals from committing more crimes]]. [[\h:red:red|There are various reasons why]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|offenders repeatedly break the law]], [[\h:red:red|but governments could certainly take steps to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|address this issue]].
**Reading the pairs**
- Every row has something on both sides — there is **no blue anywhere**. That is the signature of this type: it asks you to analyse, not to take a side.
- Rows 4 and 5 are the two questions in the prompt, in the same order. Sentence 2 answers them by naming both moves (`reasons ... but ... steps`) and not one actual reason or measure.
- `It is true that` is not a stance: it concedes a fact so the next paragraph can go on to explain it.

> ⚠ Two questions in the prompt ⇒ two answers in the body. Answer only `why` and drop `what measures`, and Task Response is capped.

> ⚠ `deter` takes `from + doing`: `deter criminals from committing more crimes`, never `deter criminals to commit`.


## Two-part question
**Question**
> **As most people spend a major part of their adult life at work, job satisfaction is an important element of individual well-being.** What factors contribute to job satisfaction? How realistic is the expectation of job satisfaction for all workers?
**Plan**
- Topic: job satisfaction
- Answer: several factors, unrealistic / impossible
**Introduction — paired against the question, line by line**
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|As most people spend a major part of their adult life at work]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|Work plays a central role in our lives]]
- [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|job satisfaction is an important element of individual well-being]] → [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|we would all like to feel fulfilled professionally]]
- [[\h:red:red|What factors contribute to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|job satisfaction]]? → [[\h:red:red|While a variety of factors may lead to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|job satisfaction]]
- [[\h:red:red|How realistic is]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the expectation of job satisfaction for all workers]]? → [[\h:red:red|it would be]] [[\h:blue:blue|unrealistic]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|to expect everyone to be happy at work]]
**Whole introduction**
> [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|Work plays a central role in our lives]], [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|and we would all like to feel fulfilled professionally]]. [[\h:red:red|While a variety of factors may lead to]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|job satisfaction]], [[\h:red:red|it would be]] [[\h:blue:blue|unrealistic]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|to expect everyone to be happy at work]].
**Reading the pairs**
- Row 1 pairs with the prompt's **first sentence** — which is background, not a question. Most candidates start at `What factors` and throw away a ready-made paraphrase: that whole clause becomes your sentence 1.
- Rows 3 and 4 are the two questions, in order. Each gets one clause of sentence 2, joined by `While ...,` — so one sentence answers both.
- Row 4 is the odd one. `How realistic ...?` does not ask yes or no, it asks for a **degree**, so the answer is the adjective itself: `unrealistic` (the Plan also allows `impossible`). That is the only blue in the paragraph.
- `feel fulfilled professionally` is a collocation — `fulfilled` + `professionally`, i.e. in your working life, not `fulfilled in job`.

> ⚠ Two questions ⇒ two answers in the body. Answer only `what factors` and drop `how realistic`, and Task Response is capped — the same trap as Problem and solution, except the two questions here are unequal: the first is open, the second is a judgement.
