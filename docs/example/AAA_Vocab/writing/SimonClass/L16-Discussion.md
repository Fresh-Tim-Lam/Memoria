# Discussion
> discuss both views, then give my own view

## Step 1 — Read and understand the question
- highlight / underline key parts

**Question**
Some people think that a sense of [[\h:green:green|competition in children]] should be encouraged. Others believe that children who are taught to [[\h:green:green|co-operate]] rather than compete become more useful adults.
**Discuss** [[\h:red:red|both]] these [[\h:red:red|views]] and [[\h:red:red|give your own opinion]].

## Step 2 — Plan your essay structure (4 paragraphs)
1. Introduction: competition or co-operation? — benefits of both, co-operation more important
2. Why encourage competition
3. Why teach co-operation (my view)
4. Conclusion: accept both views, co-operation better

## Step 3 — Planning ideas for the two main paragraphs (6 minutes)
### Why encourage competition
1. motivation to work harder
2. be better than other children
3. self confidence, independent work, faster progress
4. competitive situations when leave school e.g. job interviews
5. prepared for adult life

### Why teach co-operation (my view)
1. co-operation even more important
2. e.g. at work (teams, follow boss's instructions, help junior staff)
3. collaboration more useful than winning
4. better attitude for young people
5. working together

## Step 4 — Write the essay

### Introduction
> **Plan** — Topic / Answer
> - Topic — competition or co-operation?
> - Answer — benefits of both, co-operation more important

People have different views about whether children should be taught to be **competitive** or **co-operative**. While [[\h:red:red|**a spirit of**]]** competition** can sometimes be **useful in life**, I **believe** that **the ability to co-operate** is **more important**.
> Both halves come back in paraphrase: `competition in children should be encouraged` → `a spirit of competition can sometimes be useful in life`, `taught to co-operate` → `the ability to co-operate`; the answer `benefits of both, co-operation more important` is carried by `While … can sometimes be useful` + `more important`.

### Why encourage competition
> **Plan** — one sentence per animation state, in the order of the Step 3 brainstorm; each heading below is the coloured line printed on that slide

**Main body**

**State 1 — motivation (#1)**
On the one hand, competition can be a [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|a great source of motivation]] for children.
> Topic sentence. `On the one hand` is left unpaired on purpose: `On the other hand` opens paragraph 3.

**State 2 — work harder, be better than other children (#2)**
When teachers use games or prizes to introduce an element of competitiveness into lessons, it can encourage children to work harder [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|to outdo the other pupils]] in the class.
> `games or prizes` and `introduce … into lessons` are the concrete school setting; `outdo` is softer than `beat` and fits children.

**State 3 — self confidence, independent work, faster progress (#3)**
This kind of [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|**healthy rivalry**]]** **may help to build children's self confidence, while pushing them to work independently and progress more quickly.
> `healthy rivalry` is what keeps the paragraph positive; one `while` clause carries the remaining two brainstorm points.

**State 4 — competitive situations when leave school e.g. job interviews (#4)**
When these children leave school, their [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|confidence and determination]] will help them in competitive situations such as job interviews.
> `such as job interviews` is the only illustration in the paragraph, taken straight from the idea list.

**State 5 — prepared for adult life (#5)**
[[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|It can therefore be argued that]] competition should be encouraged in order to prepare children for adult life.
> Reuses the prompt's own verb `should be encouraged`, so the paragraph ends where the task began.

### Why teach co-operation (my view)
> **Plan** — one sentence per animation state; each heading below is the coloured line printed on that slide

**Main body**

**State 1 — co-operation is more important (#1)**
On the other hand, it is [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|perhaps even more important]] to prepare children for the many aspects of adult life that require co-operation.
> `On the other hand` answers paragraph 2 and starts my view; `perhaps even` hedges instead of repeating `I believe`.

**State 2 — e.g. at work (teams, follow boss's instructions, help junior staff) (#2)**
In the workplace, adults are expected to **work in teams**, **follow instructions **given by their [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|superiors]], or **supervise and support **the more junior members of staff.
> Three verb phrases (`work in teams`, `follow instructions`, `supervise and support`) turn one brainstorm point into a list of duties.

**State 3 — collaboration more useful than winning (#3)**
Team collaboration skills are much more useful than [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|a **competitive determination** to win]].
> A direct comparison between the two camps, built on the same abstract-noun shape as `healthy rivalry`.

**State 4 — better attitude for young people (#4)**
This is the attitude that I believe schools should [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|foster]] in young people.
> `foster` = 培养（attitude 之类看不见的东西）; `This is the attitude that …` points straight back to `collaboration skills` in state 3.
**State 5 — working together (#5)**
Instead of promoting the idea that people are either winners or losers, teachers could show children that they [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|gain more from working together]].
> A negative closing move: `Instead of promoting …` rejects the competition camp by name (`winners or losers`), and the suggestion lands on `teachers`.

## Conclusion

> **Plan** — accept both views ✅, then say which side wins (co-operation better)

In conclusion, **I can understand why** people might want to encourage competitiveness in children, but it seems to me that a co-operative attitude is [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|much more desirable]] in adult life.
> Discussion 结论的固定套式：`I can understand why … but it seems to me that …` —— 先让步接受对方观点，再给自己的判断；不引入任何新论点，只回收两段主张。

## Vocabulary

- competition / co-operation, competitive, co-operative, co-operate, competitiveness
- a spirit of competition
- a source of motivation
- introduce an element of competitiveness
- outdo other pupils, healthy rivalry
- build self confidence, determination
- aspects of adult life
- superiors, junior members of staff
- team collaboration skills
- attitude that schools should foster
- promoting the idea
- ~~a co-operative attitude is more desirable~~
> 第 1 条是本题的**同根词家族**：`compete` → `competitive`（形容词）→ `competitiveness`（名词），`co-operate` → `co-operative`（形容词）→ `co-operation`（名词）。写作时同根换形，别整段只有 `competition`。
> `a spirit of competition` 是 Introduction 改写题目的说法，`a source of motivation` 是第二段 Topic sentence 的落点，两者都是「抽象名词 + of + 抽象名词」。
> `introduce an element of competitiveness` —— `element` 这里用单数，完整搭配是 `introduce … into …`（原句 `introduce an element of competitiveness into lessons`）。
> `outdo other pupils` 与 `healthy rivalry` 是一组：前者是动作，后者是把这种动作正面化的说法；`outdo` 比 `beat` 软，适合写孩子。
> `build self confidence, determination` —— 课件不加连字符写 `self confidence`，英式常见；`determination` 是不可数，别写 `a determination`。
> `aspects of adult life` 是第三段的骨架名词，整块可背 `the many aspects of adult life that require co-operation`。
> `superiors` 与 `junior members of staff` 是一对职场称呼（上级 / 下级），比 `boss`、`employees` 正式，适合 Task 2 语域；`staff` 本身不可数。
> `team collaboration skills` 与 `attitude that schools should foster` 分别占第三段 State 3 的主语和 State 4 的谓语位置；`foster` 后接 attitude / skill / relationship，不接人。
> `promoting the idea` = 宣扬一种观念，是 State 5 的动词短语；最后一条在课件上被划掉，因为它已是完整句子而非搭配，不必单独记。
