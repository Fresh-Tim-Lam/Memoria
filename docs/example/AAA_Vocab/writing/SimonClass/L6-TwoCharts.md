# Two different charts

## Two combinations
- **Bar chart + Pie chart**
- **Line graph + Pie chart**
- **Line graph + Table**

> slides 1-3: 'this kind of question' / 'or this kind of question' / 'or this kind of question' - chart 1 varies (bar or line), chart 2 is a pie or a table

## Key rule: don't compare them together
- **These are questions with 2 different charts, which you can't compare together**
> why not: the 2 charts usually measure different things (a trend over time vs a share of a total), so there is no common dimension - any 'chart A is higher than chart B' sentence is nonsense
> the line with L4/L5: there the comparison happens **inside one set of data** (UK vs New Zealand, France vs Italy); here it happens **inside each chart** only

## Job: 3 verbs + 1 exemption
- **Your job is to select, describe and compare the key numbers.**
- **But you don't need to compare the 2 charts together - you can describe them separately.**
> `select`: pick only the highest, lowest and most unusual numbers in each chart - the same rule as L5 (10 numbers out of 20)
> `compare`: only inside one chart (top vs bottom of chart A, the 2 extremes of chart B), never across charts
> `describe them separately`: each chart gets its own paragraph - the biggest writing difference from L4/L5

## Step 1: overview
- **First, look for one main feature in each chart** (summary / overview paragraph)
> 2 charts => **2** main features => 2 overview sentences, one per chart (the 2 sentences / 2 main points rule from L4/L5)
> common mistake: an overview for the line graph only, with the pie chart or table left as decoration - that loses half the task

## Step 2: details
- **Second, describe specific numbers for each chart separately** ('details' paragraphs)
> one paragraph per chart, or two if one chart is data-heavy; keep the paragraph order the same as the 2 overview sentences

## Task: bar chart + pie chart

![Task](<.memoria/images/微信图片_20261021210255_922_77.png> "width=800,align=center")
> bar chart: men / women x 4 evening courses, 2009; pie chart: 5 age groups
> the two charts share no dimension - that is why they are described separately

### Introduction - paraphrase the question

[[\h:red:red|The bar chart below shows]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the numbers of men and women]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|attending various evening courses]] [[\h:blue:blue|at an adult education centre]] [[\h:green:green|in the year 2009]]. [[\h:red:red|The pie chart gives information about]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the ages]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|of these course participants]].

[[\h:red:red|The bar chart compares]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the numbers of males and females]] [[\h:blue:blue|who took four different]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|evening classes]] [[\h:green:green|in 2009]], and [[\h:red:red|the pie chart shows]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|the age profile]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|of these attendees]].

> shows → compares; but the second chart cannot use compares again - it switches to shows, so the 2 charts split the 2 verbs
> men and women → males and females (written register); participants → attendees; ages → **age profile** (one word replaces 'of these course participants')
> attending various evening courses → who took four different evening classes: various is vague, four different is the real number on the chart
> at an adult education centre - delete it: the place takes no part in the comparison, it is wasted words
> in the year 2009 → in 2009: the year does not need 'the year' to prop it up

### Overview - 2 sentences, 2 main points

> 2 sentences = 2 charts: `It is clear that` carries the bar chart, `We can also see that` carries the pie chart - one feature each, never a comparison between the two

It is clear that **significantly more women than men** attended evening classes at the education centre.
We can also see that evening courses were **much more popular among older adults**.

### Details - chart 1: bar chart

> one paragraph, chart 1 only - the pie chart gets its own paragraph next

According to the bar chart, drama, **painting **and **language** courses all [[\h:green:green|attracted]] **more women than men** to the **education centre** in **2009**. 
**Language **classes ***had the highest numbe***r of participants overall, [[\h:red:red|with ]]**40 female and 20 male** students, while **painting **was a popular choice among **both genders**, [[\h:red:red|attracting ]]**30 female and 25 male** [[\h:blue:blue|attendees]]. 
The only course with a higher number of **males **was **sculpture**, but this course was taken by a mere **15 people in total.**

### Details - chart 2: pie chart

> same shape as chart 1: one sentence for the headline feature, then `To be precise` for the numbers

Looking at the **age profile **pie chart, we can see that **the **[[\h:red:red|**majority **]]**of people attending evening lessons were [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|over 40 years of age]]**.
To be precise, **42% of them were [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|aged 50 or more]]**, and **26% were aged between 40 and 49**.
Younger adults were[[\h:green:green| **in the minority**]], [[\h:blue:blue|with ]]only **11% of students aged 20 to 29**, and only **5% [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|aged under 20]]**.

> the 4 ways this paragraph dates a range: `aged 50 or more` / `aged between 40 and 49` / `aged 20 to 29` / `aged under 20` - only the range word changes, `aged` stays
> `over 40 years of age` is the overview phrase **older adults** made countable - that is the job of the details paragraph

### Vocabulary: paraphrasing, comparing, describing changes
- attending courses, who took classes
- participants, attendees, students
- males and females
- courses attracted more women
- a popular choice among both genders
- significantly more, higher, highest
- had the highest number, with (+ number)
- was taken by a mere 15 people
- According to the bar chart,
- Looking at the age profile pie chart,
- the majority of people
- were in the minority
- To be precise, …
