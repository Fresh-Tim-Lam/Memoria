# Process diagram
> - **Process diagrams show steps or stages in a process**
> - **e.g. how glass is made, how a type of food is produced** - a product being made
> - **natural cycles e.g. water cycle, life cycle of a butterfly** - a cycle that returns to its own start

![ScreenShot_2026-10-23_091846_341](<.memoria/images/ScreenShot_2026-10-23_091846_341.png> "width=580,align=center")

> - **Describing a process is completely different from describing a graph, bar chart, pie chart or table**

## Differences: 
- no numbers 
- can't compare anything 
- can't describe trends (increase, decrease)
## So, what can we describe?
- how many steps
- where the process begins and ends
- each step in the process


## Language for process descriptions
- 'steps' language
> - At the first stage in the process
> - The process begins with
> - Secondly, Finally
> - The second step involves
> - Next, then, after that
> - At the following stage
- passive verbs

## Task: glass containers

![Writing task: how glass containers, such as bottles, are produced and recycled](<.memoria/images/l7-task-glass-containers.png> "width=560,align=center")
> the question is a **two-part** diagram, not one process: the top strip ends at `Glass containers`, the bottom strip starts again from `used glass`
> glass production runs Mixing -> Melting -> Shaping -> Glass containers; glass recycling runs Collection of used glass -> Sorting by colour -> Washing -> Crushing and melting -> Moulding into new products
> the step-by-step shots below follow a simplified six-step version, so their step numbers do not map one-to-one onto the five boxes of the recycling strip here

### Introduction - paraphrase the question

**The question:** [[\h:red:red|The diagrams below show]] [[\h:blue:blue|how]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|glass containers, such as bottles]], [[\h:blue:blue|are produced and recycled]].

**The introduction:** [[\h:red:red|The first flow diagram illustrates the process of]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|glass container production]], [[\h:red:red|and the second diagram shows steps in the process of]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|recycling used glass]].


### Overview - 2 sentences, 2 main points
We can see that glass is made using **three main raw materials**, and that the manufacturing process consists of [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|four distinct stages]].
It requires **five steps** to [[\h:#1e3a8a:#bfdbfe|turn used glass into new glass products]].
> one sentence per diagram, and the counting itself is the answer - materials + stages for diagram 1, steps for diagram 2
> no step is named and no trend verb appears; the reusable frames are `consists of N distinct stages` and `It requires N steps to turn A into B`

## Paragraph 3: steps

At [[\h:red:red|the first stage]] in the production of glass, sand, soda ash, limestone and other chemicals [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|are mixed together]]. [[\h:red:red|Next]], this mixture [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|is heated]] in a glass furnace [[\h:green:green|at approximately 1500°C]] [[\h:green:green|to produce molten glass]]. The molten glass can [[\h:red:red|then]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|be shaped]], by blowing, [[\h:green:green|to create the end products]], [[\h:green:green|namely glass containers]].
> three sentences = three steps: mixing, heating in the furnace, shaping by blowing; the diagram's fourth box (`Glass containers`) is the product, so it rides in on `namely glass containers` instead of getting a sentence of its own
> the three raw materials the overview counted are named here - sand, soda ash, limestone - and `other chemicals` is the catch-all that keeps the list honest
> one connector per sentence, each in a different position: `At the first stage` opens sentence 1, `Next` starts sentence 2, `then` sits **inside** sentence 3 instead of in front of it
> all three verbs are passive (`are mixed together`, `is heated`, `be shaped`): nobody in the diagram is the doer, so the glass stays the subject from start to finish
> `can then be shaped` is modal + passive - the diagram labels this step `by blowing`, so the modal says the method is one option and the participle says the glass is what gets acted on
> `by blowing` hangs off the passive as the how; `to create the end products, namely glass containers` closes the paragraph on the product, the same slot the overview left open
> this paragraph follows the **production** strip, not the six-step recycling shots above - the connectors transfer across both diagrams, the step count does not

## Paragraph 4: steps

Glass recycling [[\h:red:red|begins with]] the collection of used glass products. The collected glass [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|is sorted]] according to its colour, [[\h:red:red|and then]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|washed]] [[\h:green:green|in order to remove any impurities]]. [[\h:red:red|At the fourth stage]] of recycling, the glass [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|is crushed and melted]], and [[\h:green:green|the resulting molten glass]] can [[\h:red:red|finally]] [[\h:yellow:#b37b02|be moulded]] [[\h:green:green|to create new items]].
> five boxes in four sentences: collection / sorting + washing / crushing and melting / moulding - the sentence count and the step count differ, but the overview's `five steps` still holds
> the opening is a verb phrase this time (`begins with`), after P3 opened with a prepositional phrase (`At the first stage`) - the two paragraphs must not start the same way
> one connector per sentence, and again no two in the same slot: `begins with` sits in the verb, `and then` joins two participles, `At the fourth stage` goes in front, `finally` slips between modal and participle
> `is sorted ... and then washed` shares one `is`: the second participle drops the auxiliary, which is how two boxes stay inside one sentence
> `is crushed and melted` does the opposite - one box, two participles, one `is` - so the stage is not split in two
> `can finally be moulded` copies P3's `can then be shaped`: modal + passive is the fixed skeleton for the last step of both strips
> the boxes become verbs, not copies of the box wording: `Sorting by colour` -> `sorted according to its colour`, `Moulding into new products` -> `be moulded to create new items`
> `the resulting molten glass` squeezes the previous stage into one modifier; `in order to remove any impurities` and `to create new items` are the two purpose tails, each stage given a reason or a product

## Vocabulary: paraphrasing, steps, passive verbs

- steps / stages in the process of
- consists of four distinct stages
- it requires five steps
- raw materials, end products
- production, manufacturing
- turn used glass into new products
- at the first stage, next, then, begins with
- are mixed, is heated, can be shaped
- is sorted according to
- can be moulded to create
- are mixed -> this mixture
- collection -> the collected glass
- is melted -> the resulting molten glass
> `steps / stages in the process of`, `consists of four distinct stages` and `it requires five steps` answer the same overview question (how many); pick one and never count again in the body
> `raw materials` in, `end products` out - the two ends of any process, and the pair the first overview sentence is built on
> `production` and `manufacturing` swap freely as the head noun; `turn used glass into new products` compresses that whole production strip into one active verb
> the passive group never travels alone: `sorted according to` its colour, `moulded to create` new items - a participle plus its preposition or purpose tail is what makes it a clause
> the last three are the only real technique on the slide: the step's verb becomes a noun, and that noun opens the next sentence - which is why these three are the ones highlighted in yellow
> `are mixed -> this mixture` keeps it a bare noun with a pointer; `collection -> the collected glass` turns the step name into a participle in front of the noun; `is melted -> the resulting molten glass` makes the noun carry the whole previous stage inside it
